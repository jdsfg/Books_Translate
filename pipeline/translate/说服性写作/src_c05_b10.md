> Stanford has captured my imagination since I was a young child. Its commitment to academic excellence, its world-class faculty, and its vibrant student community make it the ideal place for me to pursue my passion for computer science.

This opening commits at least three of the named pitfalls.

**Q2. Rewrite this supplement using two specific Stanford facts.**

A candidate is applying to Stanford as a prospective CS major. They have done a summer Python project building a small recommendation engine for their school library. Write a 200-word _why Stanford_ supplement that uses at least two specific Stanford-only facts (you may invent plausible specific facts: a specific course code with title, a specific faculty member's research direction, a specific feature of Stanford's CS curriculum). The supplement should pass all six moves.

Hint

The candidate's library-project vignette is reusable. The Stanford facts are not.

**Answer:**

> Stanford CS 224W (_Machine Learning with Graphs_) is the course I would not be ready for if I applied next year, and I want the year of preparation it would take. My summer recommendation engine for my school library did the easy part, content-based filtering on book metadata, and broke when I tried to model what 320 patrons borrowed in what sequence over two years. The graphs version is what I could not figure out. Professor Leskovec's lecture notes (which I have read in pieces, mostly the parts I could mostly understand) are the closest thing I have found to a textbook for the question I was actually trying to ask. Stanford's CURIS program is the second reason I am applying: I want to do, with someone else's supervision, the kind of project I tried to do alone last summer. I would bring back to the library, both the Stanford library and my school library, whatever recommendation engine the next attempt produces. I would also like to take the metadata I have already collected to a faculty member who would know what to do with it.

This passes all six moves: Move 1 (Janet reads the candidate as having done specific work), Move 2 (the Specific Story is the broken recommendation engine), Move 3 (CS 224W is a Specific Fact placed in motion), Move 4 (six Specific Objects: 224W, 320 patrons, Professor Leskovec's lecture notes, CURIS, the school library, the metadata), Move 5 (the reciprocal-fit sentence about bringing the engine back), Move 6 (the broken-engine vignette is not generic).
