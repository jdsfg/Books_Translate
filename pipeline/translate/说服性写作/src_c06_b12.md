The rewrite engages with two faculty members specifically, names a paper and a methodological framework for one and a developmental application for the other, and connects each to the candidate's prior work. The reader can verify that the candidate has done the engagement.


#### Think Deeper

**Q1.** The chapter argues that a personal anchor (e.g., a grandmother with Alzheimer's) belongs as a brief acknowledgment, not as the opener, in a graduate statement. Some statements successfully open with a personal scene. What makes a personal opener work in this register, when it works?

**Discussion:** A personal opener can work in a graduate statement when it is _very brief_ (one to three sentences), _specifically tied to a research question,_ and _immediately followed_ by the research question in concrete form. The opener is not narrative; it is a hook that says _here is the personal entry point to a question I will now describe in research terms._ The pattern is: scene (one sentence), connection (one sentence), research question (one to two sentences). What does not work: a paragraph of personal narrative without the research question following immediately. The reader needs to know within the first 60 seconds that the document is a research document; longer personal narratives delay this signal.

**Q2.** The chapter recommends naming exactly two to three faculty in the fit paragraph. Why not name one (showing focus) or five (showing thoroughness)?

**Discussion:** One signals over-reliance on a single advisor. Graduate-level work usually requires distributed mentorship; a candidate who has identified only one possible advisor signals they have not yet imagined the program's actual structure (multiple committee members, breadth requirements, methodological cross-pollination). Five signals lack of focus or padding. Two to three is the range that signals _I have done the work to identify specific fit and have selected the most relevant among them._ The deeper observation: the number itself is less important than the specificity of engagement with each. One faculty named with deep engagement is better than five named with shallow engagement.

**Q3.** Honest acknowledgment of uncertainty (e.g., _my research interest may shift; I do not yet know if I will pursue a PhD_) is a recommended convention in the closing. Could honesty about uncertainty be misread as lack of commitment? When does honesty cross into self-undermining?

**Discussion:** The line falls roughly here: honest acknowledgment of _intellectual_ uncertainty (the question may evolve; the methods may shift; the eventual specialization is not yet set) signals research maturity. Self-undermining acknowledgment of _commitment_ uncertainty (_I might decide graduate school isn't for me;_ _I haven't really thought about whether I'm a good fit_) signals the wrong thing. The chapter's recommended honesty is the first kind. It does not say _I might not finish._ It says _I expect the specific question to evolve; I will use the program to find out._ The committee reads the first as maturity and the second as warning.
