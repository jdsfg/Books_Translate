Below is a generic supplement opening — the kind that could belong to any candidate. Rewrite it so that one Specific Object you (the reader) genuinely own, have used, have read, or have built in the past year anchors the opening. The Object should be nameable in a phrase ("a green plastic Saturday-school radio," "the second-edition copy of _Norwegian Wood_ my aunt mailed me"). Keep the rewrite to about 80 words.

> Princeton's emphasis on undergraduate research and small-class discussion is what draws me. I have always loved the intersection of literature and history, and I am looking for a program that lets me explore both rigorously. Princeton's reputation for producing thoughtful, engaged graduates makes it the natural place for me to spend the next four years.

Hint

Pick an Object that is on your desk, in your bag, or in your phone's photo roll right now. The Object should carry your fingerprints — something a different candidate could not have written about. If the Object is a book, name the edition and what page you are stuck on. If a tool, name the project it has been on.

**Answer:** Answers will vary; the discipline is in whether the Object is genuinely yours. A passing rewrite:

> The history of the Atlantic slave trade I am currently reading — Marcus Rediker's _The Slave Ship,_ the copy with the brittle library-sale sticker still on the spine — has me stuck on the chapter about the ship's surgeons, because I do not yet have the vocabulary to argue with it. Princeton's HIS 388 with Professor Sandra Greene is where I would learn the vocabulary. I would also bring back to Princeton's Africana Studies reading room the marginalia I have been keeping in the back of the Rediker.

The rewrite earns the read because the Object (the specific Rediker copy, with its sale sticker) is one this candidate alone could have placed on the page. The next round of revision should add a second Princeton-specific fact; the Object has done the harder work of making the opening _theirs_.
