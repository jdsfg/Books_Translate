### Chapter Summary

The supplement is the form most candidates underweight and most admissions readers weight heavily. Its convention is specificity: specific facts about the school that the candidate could only have learned by doing real research, paired with specific facts about the candidate that connect to those facts. Brochure language is the dominant pitfall; the AI test is harsher for supplements than for the personal essay.

The recycling matrix allows reasonable reuse of personal vignettes while keeping school-specific paragraphs fresh per school. The reciprocal-fit convention asks the candidate to claim what they would bring to the school's community, not only what the school would do for them.

The next chapter (Chapter 5) treats the graduate personal statement, where Janet is replaced by a graduate-program faculty committee with a different reading question altogether: not _who is this person?_ but _what research direction is this person prepared to pursue?_
