#### Application Problems

**Q1. Convert undergraduate to graduate register.**

A candidate's draft graduate statement opens:

> Ever since I was a child, I have been fascinated by the human brain. Watching my grandmother decline from Alzheimer's disease over the past decade has shaped my understanding of memory and identity in profound ways. As an undergraduate, I have pursued these interests through coursework in psychology, neuroscience, and philosophy.

Rewrite this opening in the graduate register. You may invent plausible specifics consistent with a candidate interested in memory research who has worked in a behavioral neuroscience lab and has co-authored a poster.

Hint

The childhood-anchor opening must go. Replace with a research question and a specific prior project.

**Answer:**

> The question I am applying to study is how spatial reorganization of memory traces during sleep is altered in early-stage Alzheimer's, and whether the alteration precedes overt cognitive decline. The question came into focus during my senior thesis in Professor Reinhart's lab, where I helped run a polysomnography study of 24 participants in the early-MCI stage and 24 age-matched controls. We found a 38% reduction in slow-wave sleep coherence between hippocampal and prefrontal sites in the MCI group, replicating recent work from the Stickgold lab but in a cohort of slightly earlier disease progression. The grandmother-with-Alzheimer's anchor (which is true; my grandmother was diagnosed when I was eleven) is not why I am applying to graduate school. The 38% finding, and what it does and does not yet imply, is.

The rewrite preserves the personal anchor as a brief acknowledgment in the second-to-last sentence rather than as the opening. The opening is the research question and the senior-thesis evidence. The candidate's identity remains visible without dominating the document.

**Q2. Diagnose the fit paragraph.**

> I would be honored to work with the distinguished faculty at MIT, including Professor X, Professor Y, and Professor Z, all of whom have made significant contributions to the field. I look forward to engaging with cutting-edge research and contributing to the vibrant intellectual community at MIT.

Identify the pitfalls and rewrite the paragraph using two specific faculty engagements.

Hint

The current version commits the faculty-name-drop pitfall and the brochure-echo pitfall.

**Answer:** Pitfalls: faculty-name-drop without engagement (X, Y, Z named but no specific reason); brochure echo (_distinguished faculty, significant contributions to the field, cutting-edge research, vibrant intellectual community_); inflated language (_I would be honored_); generic closing.

A rewrite (using specific made-up faculty for illustration):

> I would most want to work with Professor Tenenbaum, whose 2020 paper on probabilistic program induction frames a question I have been pursuing in my honors thesis on category learning under sparse data. The hierarchical-Bayesian framework his lab uses would give me the machinery to test the specific generalization question my behavioral data raise. I am also drawn to Professor Saxe's work on the developmental origins of theory of mind; her use of fMRI in 4- to 8-year-olds is exactly the methodological orientation I would like to learn, and the question of how Bayesian frameworks scale to younger participants is one I expect to encounter in my own work.
