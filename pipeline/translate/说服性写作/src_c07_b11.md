#### Think Deeper

**Q1.** The chapter argues that PhD committees are evaluating future colleagues, not just future students. Where does this evaluation actually happen in the SOP, beyond the explicit content?

**Discussion:** The future-colleague evaluation happens in four places. _Voice_ — the prose's confidence, judgment, and intellectual posture as a writer of academic English in the field. _Methodological honesty_ — the acknowledgments of what the candidate has not yet learned, which signal the self-awareness colleagues need. _Engagement with recent faculty work_ — reading the faculty as current colleagues rather than as cited authorities. _Plan-evolution acknowledgment_ — the recognition that the dissertation will evolve, which signals comfort with how research actually proceeds. Each is read as a future-colleague signal even though the SOP never says _I will be a good colleague._

**Q2.** Some PhD applicants have done one major research project; others have done several. The chapter recommends two to three pieces of evidence. What does the candidate with only one major project (typically the senior thesis) do?

**Discussion:** A single major project, developed in depth, can carry the SOP. The candidate should expand the senior-thesis paragraph to cover the original question, the methodological development (including paradigms tried that did not work), the result, what was learned beyond the thesis's expected scope, and what the candidate has begun to think since. Supplementary evidence can include a coursework project that supplied methodological tools, a summer experience that opened a different research question, or a sustained reading project — each described briefly. The candidate with one project does not need to invent additional projects; they need to develop the one project deeply enough to demonstrate the breadth of their thinking around it.

**Q3.** The chapter recommends acknowledging that the dissertation will evolve. Some applicants worry this signals lack of commitment to the proposed direction. Where does this recommendation come from?

**Discussion:** The recommendation comes from how dissertations actually develop. Faculty advisers know that the dissertation a candidate proposes in their SOP is the proposal; the actual dissertation is what the candidate produces over five to seven years of work. A candidate who refuses to acknowledge evolution signals either naivety (they have not thought about how research evolves) or rigidity (they will resist the necessary revision). Faculty want neither. The recommendation is not to be vague about the proposal, be specific about what the proposal currently is, but to acknowledge that the proposal is a plan, and plans interact with reality. The acknowledgment is itself a signal of research maturity.
